01HISTORY

What did the first screws look like?

  • Early screws were more like machines.
  • A common early type was a wooden spiral.
  • It lifted water and pressed grapes or olives.
  • Workers cut the thread by hand.
  • A thread is like a ramp around a cylinder.

01HISTORY

When were screws invented?

No one invented the screw in one day. It changed step by step.

3rd century BC

The Archimedes screw lifts water.

1400s

Metal screws are made by hand.

1797

A lathe cuts threads more evenly.

1841

Britain sets a standard thread.

Late 1940s

Metric and inch standards are defined.

02DEVELOPMENT · PART 1

How did screws become what they are today?

  • At first, workers cut each thread by hand.
  • In 1797, Maudslay's lathe cut threads more evenly.
  • In 1841, Whitworth pushed for one standard.
  • Standard threads let screws and nuts fit together.
  • Factories could then make screws in large numbers.

02DEVELOPMENT · PART 2

How did screws become what they are today?

  • Materials changed: wood, iron, steel, stainless steel.
  • A zinc coat or black finish helps stop rust.
  • Heads changed: slot, cross, hex, star.
  • Today, screws are designed for each job.

04TYPES · 1 / 3

Self-tapping screws

  • It has a sharp point and close threads.
  • It cuts its own thread in metal or plastic.
  • Hard materials may need a small pilot hole.
  • Common in computer cases, toys, and appliance covers.

04TYPES · 2 / 3

Wood screws

  • Coarse threads grip wood well.
  • The sharp point starts easily.
  • A pilot hole helps stop wood from splitting.
  • Common in furniture, wooden frames, and floors.

04TYPES · 3 / 3

Self-drilling screws

  • The tip works like a small drill.
  • It drills a hole and cuts a thread in one step.
  • Used on thin steel, metal frames, and roofs.
  • Use a drill or a power screwdriver.
